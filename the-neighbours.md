# The Neighbours

LibreOffice is one answer to "how do I
do office work"; it is not the only
inhabited house on the street. What
each neighbour trades, honestly:

## Microsoft 365

The incumbent: Word, Excel, PowerPoint
as a subscription, on the desktop and
in the browser, with OneDrive holding
the files and genuinely good
simultaneous editing. If your working
life is lived inside other people's
`.docx` files at high fidelity, or your
household's spreadsheets carry VBA
macros that must run (chapter 12), the
rent buys real things: the reference
implementation of its own formats, and
the fonts. What it costs beyond money
is the shape of the bargain — stop
paying and the best reader of your own
documents becomes a viewer. The file
formats are the hedge in both
directions: 365 reads and writes ODF
too, after its fashion.

## Google Docs

The browser suite: nothing to install,
sharing reduced to a link, several
cursors in one paragraph as a way of
life. For collaborative drafting it is
the smoothest tool most people will
ever touch. The trades: your documents
live on Google's machines in Google's
formats (exports are translations,
with the same family of losses as
chapter 8), offline is a half-feature,
and heavy documents — the 400-page
report, the 90,000-row sheet — strain
it. Many households run both: Docs for
the shared and the light, LibreOffice
for the private, the heavy, and the
permanent.

## OnlyOffice

The closest neighbour in shape: a
free suite (desktop editors, and a
server for shared editing) built
around OOXML as its native format
rather than ODF. If your world is
Word-shaped and you want a local,
unrented editor whose fidelity
loyalties run the other way from
LibreOffice's, it is the honest
alternative; its desktop editors are
free, its collaboration server is the
part with editions and licences to
read carefully.

## Calligra and the platform suites

KDE's **Calligra** (Words, Sheets,
Stage) is the other open-source suite
of stature — lighter, ODF-native,
smaller community, some genuinely
nice ideas in its docked-tool design.
macOS arrives with Apple's **Pages,
Numbers and Keynote** already
installed: pleasant, capable, and
married to Apple's own formats, from
which export is a translation again.
Neither is a mistake; both are
answers for people already home in
their worlds.

## Pandoc, the different animal

Not a suite: a command-line translator
between *text* formats — Markdown to
`.docx`, `.docx` to HTML, and thirty
more — which does not know what a
toolbar is. For documents whose
structure is the content (reports,
books, documentation), Pandoc's
translation is often cleaner than any
suite's, because it carries structure
only and drops the furniture. It
cannot help your spreadsheet at all.
The suite in this guide and Pandoc
are complements more often than
rivals: words through Pandoc, tables
and finished pages through the
engine.

## Choosing, in one paragraph

Subscribe if the formats and the
macros are your employer's and the
fidelity is non-negotiable. Share
lightly and live in a browser if that
is where your collaborators are.
Choose LibreOffice when the documents
are *yours* — private, permanent,
numerous, or numerous enough that
converting them in bulk (chapters 9
and 10) is a feature and not a
fantasy. Whichever house you pick,
keep originals in an open format
where you can: suites are mortal,
and files outlive them.
