# Impress and Draw

The honest survey chapter. The lab
exercised Writer and Calc to the metal
and opened the suite's front door; it
did not build a presentation or draw a
diagram for real, so this chapter
teaches the shape of the two remaining
big components from the documentation
and the shared frame (chapter 4) — and
labels itself accordingly rather than
borrowing authority it did not earn.

## Impress: presentations

An Impress file is a deck of **slides**,
each built on a **slide master** that
fixes the background, the title's place
and the footer for the whole deck —
the style idea of chapter 5 wearing a
projector. The working view adds a
filmstrip of slides down the left for
reordering and a main pane for the
slide itself.

What a mere mortal actually needs:

- **Layouts, not textboxes.** Each
  slide offers layouts (Title; Title
  and Content; Two Content…). Text
  typed into layout placeholders
  inherits the master and shows up in
  the outline; text in hand-drawn boxes
  is decoration that outline tools
  cannot see — the same styles lesson
  as Writer, in slide form.
- **Speaker notes** live under each
  slide (View → Notes) and print as
  notes pages; the audience never sees
  them.
- **Presenting** is F5 (from the
  start) or Shift+F5 (from this slide).
  With two screens, the Presenter
  Console shows you the next slide and
  a clock while the audience sees only
  the deck.
- **Export is the deliverable.** Decks
  travel as PDF (chapter 13's export
  puts one slide per page) far more
  safely than as `.pptx`, and a deck
  you must hand over *editable* is the
  one case for PowerPoint format —
  with chapter 8's fidelity warning in
  full: animations and transitions are
  the least portable objects in either
  suite, and fonts the second least.

Impress opens `.pptx` and saves it
back; for the text-and-boxes deck that
is most decks, the round trip is
uneventful. Build anything fancy in
the program that will present it.

## Draw: diagrams and pages

Draw is the vector drawing component:
shapes, lines with connectors that
*stay attached* when boxes move, text,
layers, and pages up to poster size.
Its honest niches:

- **Flowcharts and diagrams** — the
  connector lines are the feature;
  organisation charts, network sketches
  and floor plans are the everyday
  wins.
- **A light page-layout tool** — a
  poster, a flyer, a form: Draw's page
  is a friendlier place for them than a
  word processor fighting its own text
  flow.
- **PDF touching-up** — Draw opens a
  PDF page as editable objects. For
  fixing a word in someone else's PDF
  it is surprisingly capable; for a
  200-page document it is the wrong
  tool, and it will not preserve the
  original's text flow — treat the
  result as a new drawing, not an
  edited document.

Drawings save as `.odg` and export to
SVG and PNG cleanly, which is how most
of them leave the house.

## Base and Math, in one breath

**Base** is a front door to databases:
it can keep a small built-in database
of its own, or sit over an existing one
(MySQL, PostgreSQL, a spreadsheet at a
pinch) with forms and reports. It is a
real tool with a real learning curve;
this guide opens the door and points —
chapter 15 names fuller neighbours.
**Math** typesets formulas as objects
inside other documents (`x = {-b ± √{b²
- 4ac}} over {2a}` in its markup); if
you need it, you already know why, and
Insert → OLE Object → Formula is the
way in.

## Why these two got the short chapter

Because the lab's rule is evidence
first. Writer and Calc earned long
chapters by surviving measurement;
Impress and Draw would earn theirs the
same way, in a lab with a deck to build
and a diagram to draw. Until then, the
map above is accurate, the depths are
the suite's own help, and the label on
this chapter is the honest one.
